    Why we built Ember Atlas

    Every day satellites see thousands of fires. We wanted anyone to be able to look.

    Fires you can't see from the ground

    Most fires burn far from cameras and the news: savannas in Africa, farmland in India, forests in Siberia. From orbit, every one of them shows up as a point of heat.

    Satellites never stop looking

    Instruments such as MODIS and VIIRS pass over every place several times a day and flag any spot much hotter than its surroundings. Their record goes back to the year 2000.

    One place, one search

    Type a place and Ember Atlas tells you if it is burning today, how often it has burned before, and whether its fire season is changing.

    Eyes in orbit

    1972

    Landsat 1

    The first civilian land-imaging satellite; its archive still maps burn scars.

    1999

    Terra

    Carries MODIS. Its fire record starts in November 2000, where our trends begin.

    2002

    Aqua

    A second MODIS with an afternoon pass, so each place is seen more often.

    2011

    Suomi NPP

    VIIRS sees 375 m pixels, small enough to catch far smaller fires.

    2017

    NOAA-20 & 21

    Two more VIIRS instruments (2017, 2022) for several looks a day.

    2025

    FireSat

    The first satellite of a constellation built only to spot fires early.
